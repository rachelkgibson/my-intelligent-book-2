Concept Taxonomy

This taxonomy organizes the 204 concepts in the Technology of Robotic Design learning graph into 14 categories, ordered to match a "build first, then work backwards" curriculum: students build a real robot kit, program it, and design parts for it before circling back to the deeper theory behind why each piece works.

KIT — Identifying Kit Parts and Building Your StarterBot

Hands-on orientation to a real robot kit: identifying structural, motion, power, and fastening hardware, reading assembly instructions, and guided assembly of a complete robot. (10 concepts)

FOUND — Introduction to Robotics and Systems

Conceptual grounding for what the students just built: what robotics and automation are, the universal systems model, and categories, applications, and history of robots. (17 concepts)

PROG — Programming Fundamentals

General-purpose programming concepts taught independent of any specific hardware theory: algorithms, variables, data types, control structures, functions, debugging, and program testing/security. (15 concepts)

RBOT — Programming Your Robot

Applying programming fundamentals directly to the robot: microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, open/closed-loop control basics, and writing the first robot control programs. (12 concepts)

EDP — Designing and Building Custom Parts

The engineering design process — problem definition, brainstorming, prototyping, iteration, documentation, trade-off analysis — paired with manufacturing custom parts via additive manufacturing and 3D printing. (19 concepts)

ELEC — Electrical Fundamentals

Core electrical theory behind the robot's power system: voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's Law, AC/DC, circuit types, and electrical safety. (17 concepts)

CIRC — Electronic Components and Circuits

Physical electronic components and circuit-building skills: resistors, diodes, transistors, breadboards, soldering, multimeters, and circuit design/simulation. (17 concepts)

SENS — Sensors and Measurement

A deeper dive into sensing: precision measurement, specific sensor types, calibration, and signal processing, building on the basic sensors/actuators introduced in Programming Your Robot. (14 concepts)

MECH — Mechanical Systems

Simple machines and mechanical design concepts: levers, gears, linkages, torque, friction, and structural/chassis design. (17 concepts)

FLUID — Fluid Power Systems

Hydraulic and pneumatic systems: fluid pressure, cylinders, valves, pumps, and fluid power system design. (12 concepts)

CTRL — Control Systems and Feedback

A deeper dive into control theory building on the open/closed-loop basics from Programming Your Robot: setpoints, error signals, PID basics, stability, and motion/actuator control. (12 concepts)

DIGI — Digital Logic and Computing

What's really happening inside the microcontroller: digital vs. analog electronics, binary numbers, logic gates, and the components of a computing system (CPU, memory, I/O, interfaces). (18 concepts)

RBTP — Advanced Robot Programming

Building on the programming basics from Programming Your Robot: teleoperation, motion/path planning, arm and wheeled robot control, calibration, troubleshooting, and safety programming. (9 concepts)

BUILD — Integrating, Testing, and Maintaining a Complete Robotic System

Bringing everything together: component selection, hardware platforms, assembly, wiring, system integration, testing, and maintenance. (15 concepts)
