Chapters

This textbook is organized into 14 chapters covering 204 concepts.

Chapter Overview

  1. Identifying Kit Parts and Building Your StarterBot - Hands-on introduction where students identify kit hardware and assemble a complete robot before any theory.
  2. Introduction to Robotics and Systems - Formal vocabulary for the robot just built: robotics, automation, the universal systems model, and robot categories.
  3. Programming Fundamentals - General-purpose programming concepts (algorithms, variables, control structures, functions, debugging) independent of hardware.
  4. Programming Your Robot - Applying programming fundamentals to the robot: microcontrollers, sensors, actuators, and basic control loops.
  5. Designing and Building Custom Parts - The engineering design process applied to manufacturing custom robot parts via additive manufacturing and 3D printing.
  6. Electrical Fundamentals - The electrical theory underlying the robot's power system: voltage, current, resistance, Ohm's Law, and safety.
  7. Electronic Components and Circuit Design - Physical components and circuit-building skills: resistors, transistors, breadboarding, soldering, and circuit design.
  8. Sensors and Measurement - A deeper dive into precision measurement, specific sensor types, calibration, and signal processing.
  9. Mechanical Systems and Simple Machines - The mechanical theory behind the kit's hardware: simple machines, gears, torque, friction, and chassis design.
  10. Fluid Power: Hydraulics and Pneumatics - Hydraulic and pneumatic systems: fluid pressure, cylinders, valves, pumps, and fluid power system design.
  11. Control Systems and Feedback Loops - A deeper dive into control theory: setpoints, error signals, PID basics, stability, and motion/actuator control.
  12. Digital Logic and Computing Fundamentals - What's inside the microcontroller: digital vs. analog electronics, binary numbers, logic gates, and computing components.
  13. Advanced Robot Programming - Teleoperation, motion and path planning, arm/wheeled robot control, calibration, troubleshooting, and safety programming.
  14. Integrating, Testing, and Maintaining a Complete Robotic System - The capstone: component selection, assembly, wiring, system integration, testing, and maintenance.

How to Use This Textbook

This course follows a "build first, then work backwards" approach: you'll identify parts and assemble a complete robot in Chapter 1 before learning any underlying theory. From there, you'll program the robot and design custom parts for it, then circle back through the electrical, mechanical, sensing, control, and computing theory that explains why everything you built actually works. Chapters build on each other in this order, so it's best to work through them sequentially rather than skipping ahead.

Note: Each chapter includes a list of concepts covered. Make sure to complete prerequisites before moving to advanced chapters.
