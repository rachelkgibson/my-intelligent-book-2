Technology of Robotic Design

Title: Technology of Robotic Design

Target Audience: High school

Prerequisites: None

Course Overview

Technology of Robotic Design introduces high school students to the study of robotic systems and their real-world applications. Students apply the engineering design process while exploring programming, electrical concepts, mechanical concepts, and control systems that together form the foundation of modern robotics.

Through hands-on learning and problem-solving activities, students design, build, and program robotic systems, gaining direct experience with the same processes used by engineers and technicians in robotics, automation, and manufacturing industries. The course blends technical skill-building with professional and employability competencies, preparing students for further study or entry-level work in STEM and advanced manufacturing fields.

Robotics sits at the intersection of some of the fastest-growing career fields today, and this course gives students an early, practical foundation in the technologies driving automation across manufacturing, healthcare, agriculture, and beyond.

Main Topics Covered

  • Robotic systems and their applications
  • The engineering design process
  • Programming
  • Electrical concepts
  • Mechanical concepts
  • Control systems

Topics Not Covered

  • Advanced robotics research topics such as machine learning-driven autonomy or computer vision
  • Industrial-scale automation and PLC programming at a professional/certification level
  • Advanced fluid power system design (hydraulics/pneumatics beyond introductory concepts)
  • Professional CNC machining and manufacturing certification
  • Graduate-level control theory (e.g., state-space methods, advanced feedback control mathematics)

Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

Remember

Retrieving, recognizing, and recalling relevant knowledge from long-term memory.

  • Define robotics, automation, and control systems.
  • Identify components of robotic systems.
  • Identify sensors.
  • Identify open and closed loops in control systems.
  • Identify the primary concepts and components of mechanical systems.

Understand

Constructing meaning from instructional messages, including oral, written, and graphic communication.

  • Explain the universal systems model.
  • Describe the concepts of voltage, current, and resistance in electricity.
  • Explain the primary functions of electronic systems components.
  • Describe the purpose of a microcontroller/logic controller.
  • Explain primary concepts and components of a fluid power system.

Apply

Carrying out or using a procedure in a given situation.

  • Demonstrate precision measurement equipment and techniques.
  • Simulate control and automation systems.
  • Simulate electronic circuits.
  • Measure circuit values with a multimeter.
  • Implement basic programming procedures.
  • Demonstrate how sensors are used to control robotic technology.

Analyze

Breaking material into constituent parts and determining how the parts relate to one another and to an overall structure or purpose.

  • Research robotic hardware used in various industries.
  • Compare open and proprietary hardware components.
  • Compare analog and digital electronics for control systems.
  • Research the development and future of robotics.

Evaluate

Making judgments based on criteria and standards through checking and critiquing.

  • Select the most appropriate programming language or platform for an application.
  • Ensure the security of programs.
  • Compare and judge tradeoffs between hardware and design options for a given application.

Create

Putting elements together to form a coherent or functional whole; reorganizing elements into a new pattern or structure.

  • Design a circuit for a given purpose.
  • Design a mechanical system.
  • Design code for a given purpose.
  • Design a robot.
  • Build a robot.
  • Reengineer the design of an existing robotic system.
  • Capstone project: Design, build, and program a complete robotic system that satisfies a defined set of requirements, then evaluate and document its performance against those requirements.
